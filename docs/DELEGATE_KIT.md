# Discovery delegate kit

**Purpose:** let someone the owner trusts run the five discovery conversations,
so the pilot's core unknowns (B1–B4 on the unknowns map) get answered without
the owner doing legwork.
**Status:** draft — needs exactly one owner decision: name the delegate.

## Who qualifies

- Someone the owner personally trusts.
- Someone with their own Kathmandu network (*chinajani*) — they approach
  acquaintances, never cold strangers. A stranger asking about a business
  reads as a threat (tax office, journalist, competitor, scammer); an
  acquaintance asking over tea reads as a friend.
- Speaks Nepali; comfortable walking into shops and offices mid-afternoon.

## The five questions

Frame as advice-seeking (*"tapāīṁko bichār mā..."* — in your opinion...),
never as interrogation. Let them teach; their struggles surface on their own.

1. What took most of your time or money last month that you wish didn't?
2. Tell me about the last time something went wrong. What did you do?
3. What have you already tried, and what happened?
4. Who else do you ask when you're stuck?
5. If you had a free week of help, what would you hand off?

## Hard rules

- No pitching. No promises. You are there to learn, not to sell.
- Warmth before questions: visit 2–3 times as a genuine customer first where
  possible, or arrive via a mutual contact's introduction.
- In person, at their place, mid-afternoon (roughly 2–4pm, the slow hours).
  15–20 minutes. Accept tea if offered — declining is a small insult.
- Consent before notes: *"Is it okay if I jot this down so I remember it
  right?"*
- Identities stay with you. What comes back is anonymized: no names, no
  phone numbers, no business names.
- Everything recorded is TEST/observed — never a customer, lead, or
  commitment. Follow-ups, if any, on WhatsApp (voice notes or short calls),
  never email, never forms.

## The surprise log (per conversation — the actual deliverable)

Four lines:

1. Who (anonymized, e.g. "restaurant owner, Lazimpat") and when.
2. What they said that you didn't expect.
3. What you would have guessed before.
4. One question this raises.

In a culture of polite deflection, the surprise is the only signal you can
trust — it's the one thing they didn't rehearse.

## How results come back

The delegate sends the five surprise logs (20 lines total) to the owner, who
forwards them to the driver. Nothing else travels — no recordings, no
contact lists.

## What happens then

The driver turns the surprises into hypotheses and the smallest real test
for each. The delegate is done unless asked for a second round. If the owner
names no delegate, B1–B4 stay honestly BLOCKED_BY_MISSING_ACCESS — and that
answer itself forces the pilot thesis to change.
