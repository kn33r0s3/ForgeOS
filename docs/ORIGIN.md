## NORTH STAR

> **Expand the frontier of what can be understood, discovered, created,
> coordinated, and accomplished in reality.**

This does not name an industry, a problem, a country, a product, a
customer, or a type of outcome. It defines what kind of system Hami is
becoming: whatever reality presents, Hami becomes increasingly capable
of dealing with it. There is no permanent product category — and no
predefined category of reality Hami refuses to understand.

---

## PURPOSE

> **Remove unnecessary limits caused by ignorance, disconnection,
> inaccessible capability, missing coordination, and things that have not
> yet been created.**

In plain words: make the world more legible, more connected, and more
actionable for everyone — until people are no longer limited by what
they cannot see, cannot find, cannot reach, or do not yet know is
possible.

Hami distinguishes what is truly impossible from what is merely
currently unknown, unavailable, unconnected, uncreated, or unattempted.
That distinction is its territory.

---

## GATE

An intervention counts only with **both**:

- (a) a real person gives something up for it — money, time, trust, or
  behavior; and
- (b) the affected humans authorized it.

Capability work counts only when tied to a changed outcome in reality.
Otherwise it is labelled **"unproven"** — never presented as achievement.

"Worthwhile" is not decided by Hami. Worthwhile = valued by a real
person, evidenced by what they give up for it.

---

## OPERATING PRINCIPLES

1. **Never assume the answer already exists.** Existing solutions are one
   region of the possibility space. Hami can find, combine, adapt,
   transform, build, organize, train, create, acquire, coordinate,
   invent — or conclude something should not be done.
2. **Never assume the stated problem is the real problem.** Distinguish
   what someone says they need from what reality actually requires.
3. **Never assume the known questions are the important questions.**
   Reality should generate Hami's questions, not merely answer questions
   invented beforehand. Observe → notice what doesn't fit → formulate
   the unknown from the contradiction.
4. **Public pages carry aspiration only, never capability claims.** No
   grand claims until reality backs them. A page that exercises a
   capability must run the real engine code, proven by tests.
5. **Research is valuable only insofar as it changes capability or
   action.** Finding → implication → changed assumption → changed
   priority/capability → experiment/action → evidence. The rest is
   archive, not intelligence.

## LOOP

> **Observe → Understand → Discover → Explore → Create → Coordinate →
> Act → Verify → Learn → Expand.**

The loop does not terminate, and the next loop may concern something
completely different from the last. Hami discovers its own necessary
expansion by encountering reality.

## FOUNDATION — the six primitives

The grammar Hami uses to operate. The domain changes; the semantics don't.

- **ENTITY** — what exists (people, businesses, places, resources…).
- **RELATION** — how things connect (serves, depends on, capable of…).
- **EVENT** — what changed.
- **EVIDENCE** — why we believe it (source, observation, record…).
- **CAPABILITY** — what can actually be done.
- **ACTION** — what was proposed, authorized, and actually done.

Truth progression: possible → hypothesized → tested → supported
(supported requires evidence). Never confuse possibility, evidence,
intention, execution, or outcome. Estimated ≠ actual. Requested ≠
booked. Plan ≠ execution.

---

## SUCCESS

> **What can Hami do today that it could not yesterday, and what happened
> in reality because of it?**

Both parts are required. Capability growth with no recorded outcome is
"unproven". The measure is not pages, features, users, or documents —
it is how much valuable reality became possible because Hami existed.

---

## The five questions (practical standard)

Every serious Hami cycle answers:

1. **Reality** — what actually exists or happened?
2. **Possibility** — what could become true?
3. **Constraint** — why is it not true yet? (labelled *hypothesis* until
   evidence supports it)
4. **Intervention** — what can legitimately be done to move reality
   toward it?
5. **Outcome** — did reality actually change?

## Origin: Nepali-originated, world-facing

Standing product principle (owner-set, 2026-10-04): Hami is
Nepali-originated — built in Kathmandu, representing Nepal worldwide —
and serves everywhere equally. Origin is identity, not priority: Hami
does not discriminate or prioritize by geography. A merchant in Butwal
and a merchant in Berlin meet the same system. Building and piloting
where the builder stands (Kathmandu) is circumstance, not privilege.
Nepal is the origin, not the ceiling: one Hami system, not a Nepal Hami
plus a separate global Hami.

### Why this is strategy, not decoration

Discovery rounds 1–2 found the opening directly: every tool sold to "small
businesses" was designed for someone in San Francisco. Nepali merchants
live in conversational commerce (call-first, phone-number-as-identity,
missed-call-is-missed-order), cash on delivery, Viber/WhatsApp-first
inquiries, and a paperwork wall with no informal digital tier. A product
shaped by that reality is structurally different from a translated
San Francisco tool — and that difference is the moat.

### Expressions (in order of importance)

1. **Behavior.** Hami is not a SaaS product — not a subscription tool
   merchants log into. It is economic infrastructure: an intelligence and
   action system that lives where economic life already happens
   (conversational, phone-first, tolerant of informality, honest about
   cash). If it behaves like a San Francisco SaaS with a Nepali flag on
   top, it is costume — and merchants will smell it instantly. The
   wedge experiments (like Forge Bot) are capabilities inside the system,
   not the product.
2. **Name.** Hami (हामी — "we/us") already flies the flag. Keep it
   central; Devanagari belongs in the wordmark, not as decoration.
3. **Language.** Nepali-first where merchants read; English where the
   world reads. Never machine-translated marketing Nepali — a native
   speaker owns every public Nepali word.
4. **Story.** "Built in Kathmandu from real merchant reality, for the
   world's merchants." Open-source, built in public, documented honestly.
   The world-facing part is proof of quality, not dilution of origin.
5. **Craft.** Representing Nepal worldwide means the work has to be
   excellent — sloppy work done under a national flag embarrasses the
   flag. Tests green, docs honest, promises kept.

### Anti-patterns

- Costume branding: Nepali visuals over foreign-shaped product behavior.
- Claiming to represent Nepal before the product has served a single real
  Nepali merchant — the flag is earned by reality, not asserted.
- Treating Nepal as a priority market or a test market — origin is
  identity, not privilege. No geographic discrimination, no priority
  lane.
- Diluting origin to look "global": global reach, Nepali roots. Never the
  reverse.
- Speaking for Nepal in marketing before merchants speak for Hami in
  practice.
